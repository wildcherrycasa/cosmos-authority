# Prior art: who already signs a refusal — 2026-09-08

**This document exists because the project's headline claim was false and nobody had checked.**
From the pivot on 2026-09-05 until 2026-09-08, the README, the go-to-market doc, the ten outreach
messages and a public comment on the x402 issue tracker all said some version of *"nobody signs the
refusal… it is the record nobody else produces."*

It was not true, and it was not true before it was written. At least two shipping systems and four
IETF drafts already sign refusals, the earliest published **five months before the pivot**.

**Every quote below was fetched from its primary source and is marked with how it was verified.**
Nothing here is second-hand unless it says so.

⚠ **CORRECTION TO THIS DOCUMENT, 2026-09-08 (later the same day).** The sentence above was true about how
the work was done and false about what the artifact delivered: **this file originally contained ZERO URLs.**
It cited a filename and a line number, four draft names, and a vendor — every one of them checkable only by
a reader who already knew where to look. A citation nobody can follow is indistinguishable from one that was
invented, and this project has been told exactly that about its market claims. Every row now carries a link,
and each was **re-fetched today** rather than copied forward from the first pass. Two things that survived
the re-fetch are worth naming, because both are places I nearly introduced an error while "correcting" one:
`draft-marques-asqav-compliance-receipts` really is at **-08** (datatracker lists 00 through 08), and the
Microsoft proposal really does say *"decision: permit or deny"* — a search summary describing the toolkit's
wider Cedar engine as having five decision types is about a different component, not this receipt.

## What is actually still true

**Signed refusals exist. Refusals a *stranger* can verify do not.**

Google's Agent Payments Protocol requires a signed receipt on rejection **and defines no way to
discover the signing key.** Both halves verified:

| | |
|---|---|
| the mandate | *"Upon acceptance or rejection of the Mandate, the Verifier MUST return a signed Mandate Receipt."* |
| where | `docs/ap2/agent_authorization.md`, **line 496** — <https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/main/docs/ap2/agent_authorization.md> |
| re-fetched | 2026-09-08, quote returned **verbatim** |
| key discovery in that file | **NOT PRESENT** — no JWKS, no `.well-known`, no key-discovery procedure, and no statement of how a verifier obtains the signing public key. Confirmed twice: by grep on the first pass, and by re-reading the fetched document today. |
| consequence | trust is bilateral — a verifier must establish a relationship with every agent provider, so an outsider cannot check a rejection |
| ⚠ scope | this is one file. AP2's `specification.md` carries a **second** MUST of the same shape for the Payment Mandate, so the requirement is not incidental to one document — <https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md> |

That gap is the only defensible remaining claim. Say *that*, and say it with the line number.

## Who does *not*, and why it matters

**x402 receipts are success-only, stated in the spec.** *"A receipt is a signed statement returned by
the resource server **only on success**, confirming that payment was received and service was
delivered."* (`specs/extensions/extension-offer-and-receipt.md`, line 263 — ✅ fetched.)

This is the one place the original instinct was right, and it is the premise the
`x402/evc-verifier.js` work rests on: their closed verdict schema gives a **deny** a free-form
`detail` object and gives an **allow** nowhere to put evidence at all.

## What this does to the pitch

- ❌ **Delete** *"nobody signs the refusal"* and *"the record nobody else produces"* wherever they appear.
  They are one search away from being disproved by exactly the people we are trying to reach, and two
  of them already engaged with us in good faith on that framing.
- ✅ **Keep** the narrower, checkable claim: *Google mandates a signed rejection receipt and defines no
  key discovery, so a stranger cannot verify one. Cosmos publishes the key.*
- ⚠ **The gap is months wide, not years.** One adjacent product already publishes offline-verifiable
  signed receipts for **approved** payments and keeps denials in a private ledger; that is one product
  decision away from closing this.

## Demand, measured the same day

Four independent searches, four negatives. Recorded here because the positioning correction and the
demand answer arrived together and should be read together.

⚠⚠ **READ THE COLUMN HEADING BEFORE THE ROWS. THESE ARE NOT SOURCED CLAIMS AND MUST NEVER BE QUOTED AS
THOUGH THEY WERE.** Every row below is a statement that something was *not found*. A negative claim of this
shape cannot be sourced — there is no document that says "no insurer asks for this" — so what is recorded is
**the outcome of a search, not a fact about the world.** That distinction is the whole difference between
research and a market claim invented to fill a row, and it is the part of this file most likely to be
misread as the second. Treated correctly these rows are a reason to go and ask ten people; treated as
findings they are exactly the failure this document was written to correct. **Absence of evidence is
recorded here. Evidence of absence is not claimed.**

| segment | WHAT THE SEARCH RETURNED (not: what is true) |
|---|---|
| binding regulation | no regulation found that requires it. The one checkable sub-claim: agentic payment authorization does not appear in the EU AI Act's Annex III high-risk list — <https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/> |
| auditors / assessors | no clause found in ISO 42001, SOC 2 or AIUC-1 requiring refusal evidence. ⚠ ISO 42001 and SOC 2 criteria are **paywalled and were not read in full**, so this row is bounded by what is publicly readable |
| insurers | none found asking for denial evidence. ⚠ Insurer underwriting requirements are largely **not published**; a search cannot see them, so this row is close to uninformative and should not be leaned on |
| real incidents | no public case found where anyone had to prove an AI agent's spend was **refused** |

The closest real analogue found, and it is a genuine legal duty to evidence a refusal under a stated
policy at a stated time: **OFAC 31 CFR 501.604** requires reporting a *rejected* transaction within 10
business days, including the legal authority under which it was rejected. Different domain, right shape.
⚠ **STILL agent-reported and STILL not re-fetched at source.** It is left in with that label rather than
dropped, because a labelled unverified claim is useful and an unlabelled one is a liability — but it must
not be quoted anywhere outside this file until somebody opens the CFR and reads it.
